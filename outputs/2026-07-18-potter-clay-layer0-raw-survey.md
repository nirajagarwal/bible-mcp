**The core cluster: the potter has no noun.** Hebrew has no freestanding word for "potter": the potter is *yotser*, the qal participle of יָצַר *yatsar* (H3335), "the forming one." H3335 occurs 44 times, and Isaiah owns it — 23 of 44 occurrences, with Psalms next at 7, Jeremiah 5, Genesis 3. The gloss range is dominated by participial forms ("the.[one]," "[who].formed," "[is.the.one.who].formed"), which is the finding: God-as-potter and God-as-creator are one word, and whether a translator writes "potter" or "Creator" at any occurrence is a rendering choice, not a lexical one. The corpus tagging makes the same point from the other side: at Jer 18:4 and Ps 2:9 the participle is split out as its own lemma יוֹצֵר H3335a "the.potter," while at Isa 64:7 (MT; English 64:8) the identical participle stays under verb H3335, glossed "[are the.one.who].formed.us" — the same word crossing the potter/Creator line inside one tagging scheme.

**The root's double life.** One step to the noun יֵצֶר H3336 (9x) was the richest neighbor move: its gloss range runs inclination (5 forms), form (2), and — once — "a.pot" (Isa 29:16). The same noun is the evil "inclination" of every thought in Gen 6:5 and 8:21, the "frame" God remembers is dust in Ps 103:14, and the physical pot of Isaiah's polemic. The Talmudic reading of Gen 2:7's doubled yod as the two *yetsarim* is midrash, not philology — but the consonantal fact underneath it is corpus-verified: Gen 2:7 has וַיִּיצֶר (double yod) for the forming of the man, and the control at Gen 2:19 has וַיִּצֶר (single yod) for the forming of the beasts. One further step, H3338 *yetsurim* "members" (Job 17:7, 1x), added little; the chain was ended there per the stopping rule.

**Clay hides behind a homograph.** A bare query on H2563 חֹמֶר returns only the dry-measure *homer* (13x); clay-*chomer* lives under **H2563b** (17x — glossed clay 9, mud 3, mortar 3), and H2563a is a third homograph ("heap," Hab 3:15, 1x). The H2563b distribution is a discovery about ownership: **Job 7, Isaiah 5, Jeremiah 2**. Isaiah owns the potter; *Job owns the clay* — and only in Job does the clay speak in the first person: "Remember that You molded me like clay. Would You now return me to dust?" (10:9); "I was also formed from clay" (33:6, Elihu leveling himself with Job); plus houses of clay (4:19), defenses of clay (13:12), clay under the seal (38:14). The same word is Babel's mortar (Gen 11:3) and Egypt's brick-slavery (Exod 1:14). The other clay words sort by register: חֶרֶשׂ H2789 (17x) is the fired thing — earthenware and potsherd — and Leviticus owns it (5x), always as the vessel that must be *broken* when defiled (Lev 11:33, 15:12); its poetic uses are dryness and fragment (Job 2:8; Ps 22:15 [MT 22:16]). טִיט H2916 (13x) is trodden mire — and Isa 41:25 puts the potter in the conquest register: the one from the north tramples rulers "like a potter who treads the clay." In Aramaic the whole vocabulary belongs to Daniel 2: חֲסַף (H2635, 9x, all Dan 2) with טִין "miry" (H2917), against a single Aramaic potter פֶּחָר (H6353, Dan 2:41). By English gloss, Daniel is the top "clay" book in the whole Bible (17 tokens) — clay there is not creatureliness but the brittleness of empire.

**The Greek side is tiny, and the roles have changed.** κεραμεύς "potter" occurs 3 times: Rom 9:21 and twice in Matt 27, where the potter appears only as the former owner of a field bought with blood money. κεραμικός once (Rev 2:27, quoting Ps 2:9's כְלִי יוֹצֵר shattering). πλάσσω, the LXX's verb for *yatsar*, survives twice (Rom 9:20 "will the thing formed [πλάσμα] say to him who formed it"; 1 Tim 2:13 "Adam was formed first") and ὀστράκινος "earthen" twice (2 Cor 4:7 treasure in clay vessels; 2 Tim 2:20 vessels of honor and dishonor). But πηλός "clay" occurs 6 times, and **five of the six are John 9** — Jesus making mud to open a blind man's eyes. In the NT, God never sits at the wheel: the potter survives only inside quotation and argument, while the one new clay-act in the corpus is remedial, clay applied *to* a man rather than a man made *from* clay. (Reading John 9 as a deliberate echo of Gen 2:7 is patristic tradition, not corpus data; the corpus gives only the shared word.) One more Greek thread is internal to Paul: the "lump" of Rom 9:21 is φύραμα (G5445, 5x), and its other Romans occurrence is 11:16 — "if the firstfruit is holy, so is the lump" — the same lump moving from wrath-and-mercy vessels to consecrated dough within one letter; its remaining uses are all leaven sayings (1 Cor 5:6-7; Gal 5:9).

**Register map.** The image does at least seven different jobs. (1) *Polemic against the questioning creature*: Isa 29:16 ("shall what is formed say of him who formed it, 'He has no understanding'?"), Isa 45:9-11, Rom 9:20-21, Wis 15:7 — and IMITATION.51.5 quotes it straight: "For how shall the clay boast against Him that fashioned it?" (2) *Covenant plasticity*: Jer 18:1-6, the wet-clay oracle — "the vessel... became flawed [וְנִשְׁחַת, niphal of שָׁחַת H7843] in his hand; so he formed it into another vessel." The corpus lemma for "marred" is the destroy/corrupt verb, and the whole point is that at the wheel, marring is not final. (3) *Petition*: Isa 64:8 (MT 64:7) — "we are the clay, and You are the potter" — the only place the clay speaks *as* prayer rather than under rebuke; Job 10:9 is its private twin. Cross-references confirm this verse as the theme's hub (linked by voters to Jer 18, Isa 29:16, 45:9, Rom 9, Ps 119:73, Eph 2:10), and find_similar makes its nearest neighbor Sir 33:13 (0.718). (4) *Shattering of the fired vessel*: Ps 2:9 → Rev 2:26-27; Isa 30:14 (no shard left big enough to scoop coals); Jer 19:11 ("like one shatters a potter's jar that can never again be repaired"); Jer 22:28 (Coniah a despised pot); Lam 4:2 (Zion's sons, gold re-valued as potter's jars); Hos 8:8. Jer 18 and Jer 19 are a staged pair: the same prophet buys wet clay reworked at the wheel, then a fired jar smashed at Topheth — reversibility ends at the kiln. (5) *Frailty holding treasure*: Job 4:19's "houses of clay" (quoted at length in 1CLEMENT.39.1), 2 Cor 4:7, 2 Tim 2:20-21, Ps 22:15's dried potsherd. (6) *Craft and economy*: Sir 38:29-34's potter at his kick-wheel, excluded from council yet "they maintain the fabric of the age; their prayer is in the handiwork of their craft"; the royal potters' guild at Netaim and Gederah (1 Chr 4:23); Leviticus's breakable purity vessels. (7) *The betrayal price*: Zech 11:13's thirty pieces thrown "to the potter" in the LORD's house → Matt 27:7-10. Both named potter-places in the corpus end as graveyards: Topheth (8 mentions, all 2 Kgs 23 + Jer 7 and 19) and Akeldama/potter's field (Matt 27:8; Acts 1:19).

**The Apocrypha is a load-bearing layer here.** Wis 15:7 states Rom 9:21's exact argument a step earlier — one clay, vessels for clean uses and their contrary, "the potter is the judge" — then turns it against the idol-maker: the potter, "having but a little before been made of earth," molds a god from the same clay and must soon "give back the soul which was lent him" (15:8). Sir 33:13 gives the determinist maximum ("as the clay of the potter in his hand... so men are in the hand of him who made them"), immediately after Adam-from-dust (33:10). Sir 27:5 supplies the kiln-test proverb; 2 Esd 8:2 the earth's own economics ("much clay from which earthen vessels are made, but little dust that gold comes from"). That Paul knew Wisdom is standard scholarship, not corpus data — but the verbal skeleton (same clay, two contrary uses, potter as judge) is visible in the corpus text itself.

**The prose classics keep the clay and drop the potter.** Augustine uses the potter's wheel to argue about *time* (CONFESSIONS.11.29 — would the wheel's turning still measure time if the heavens stopped?), and pointedly rejects the artificer analogy for creation: God is not like a human maker who "invests with a form what already existeth... as clay, or stone" (11.7) — ex nihilo against the potter image. Yet the incarnation is "a lowly habitation of our clay" (7.24), and Monica delays his baptism preferring "to expose to them the clay whence I might afterwards be moulded, than the very cast, when made" (1.18). Julian uses clay to mark the body/soul frontier: the body from "the clay of earth," but for the soul God "would take right nought" (JULIAN.62.7). Chesterton's sculptor "is glad that the clay is colourless" — creation loves its limits (ORTHODOXY.3.23). Bunyan has one glancing "vessels of clay" (PILGRIM.1.616). Brother Lawrence (PRESENCE) and Barnabas: zero hits — a genuine layer gap, stated as such.

**Synthesis question.** The corpus leaves one live wire exposed: **can the vessel change its use — is honor assigned at the wheel (Rom 9:21; Sir 33:13; Wis 15:7), or attainable by the vessel's own purging (2 Tim 2:20-21, where "if anyone purges himself he will be a vessel for honor," in the same σκεῦος-εἰς-τιμήν vocabulary), and is Jer 18's re-made pot the potter's prerogative or the nation's repentance (18:8, 18:11)?**

2. Gen 2:7 וַיִּיצֶר (double yod) vs Gen 2:19 וַיִּצֶר (single yod) — verified this run via interlinear; control pair.

4. Corpus tags the potter-participle inconsistently: H3335a at Jer 18:4 and Ps 2:9, plain H3335 at Isa 64:7 (MT) — checkable via those three interlinears.
5. Five of six NT πηλός occurrences are in John 9; the sixth is Rom 9:21 — `word_study G4081`.

## Gaps noticed

- **get_citations recall gap, confirmed instance**: 1CLEMENT.39.1 contains an extended verbatim quotation of Job 4:19-21, but `get_citations("Job 4:19")` returns empty (as do Isa 64:8 and Rom 9:21). Full-text search within the patristic works remains the real probe.
- **Isaiah 64 interlinear off-by-one (documented bug, not a discovery)**: `get_interlinear("Isaiah 64:8")` shows the English text of 64:8 but the Hebrew tokens of MT 64:8 = English 64:9 ("Do not be angry, O LORD"); the clay verse's Hebrew must be fetched as 64:7. English/MT offset outside Psalms; within Psalms the corpus instead displays dual numbering cleanly (e.g. "Ps.22.16 = Ps.22.15").
- **Homograph discoverability**: nothing in the H2563 output signals that H2563a/H2563b exist; a surveyor who didn't already suspect the clay sense would file *chomer* as a dry measure and lose the theme's densest Hebrew noun.
- **English-gloss word_study noise**: `word_study("clay")` includes 6 tokens of H0001b — the Aramaic determiner א picked up as a "clay" gloss carrier — inflating Daniel's count; a tokenization artifact.
- **Sample-verse bleed in word_study**: sample lists include verses where the queried sense is invisible in translation (Lev 6:21 griddle under H2789; Hab 3:15 "churning" under H2563a; Job 41:22 under both H2789 and H2916), so sample verses cannot be trusted as attestations without an interlinear check.
- **No LXX layer**: MT/LXX forks central to this theme (Zech 11:13 potter/foundry; LXX Isa 45:9's divergent reading) cannot be checked; DanGr is the only Greek-OT witness present and merely duplicates Daniel results.
- **semantic_search lexical hijack, one instance**: "the potter shaping a vessel on the wheel" surfaced Ezekiel's merkabah wheels and 1 Kgs laver wheels above most potter texts; also one JULIAN glossary line ("_Wretch_ = a poor, a mean creature") as pure noise.
- **Thin layers as findings**: PRESENCE (Brother Lawrence) and BARNABAS have zero potter/clay hits; the theme's prose weight sits almost entirely in CONFESSIONS and IMITATION.
