**The phrase and its camouflage.** The exact phrase "living water" occurs only 7 times in a BSB full-text search: John 7:38; John 4:10, 11; Jer 2:13; Jer 17:13; Zech 14:8; Rev 7:17. But the Hebrew phrase *mayim chayyim* (H4325 + H2416a) is wider than English lets on. Interlinear checks confirm the identical construction — noun *mayim* + adjective *chayyim* (parsed Aampa, "living") — at Gen 26:19 (BSB "a well of **flowing** water"), Lev 14:5-6 (BSB "**running** water" / "the **fresh** water"), Num 19:17 (BSB "**fresh** water"), and Song 4:15 (BSB "a well of **fresh** water"). The corpus data thus shows the English translation itself performing a register split the Hebrew does not make: where the water is ritual or erotic, translators write "fresh/running"; where it is theological, they write "living." A keyword survey of the English text silently loses the ritual half of the theme. That is a finding about method as much as about the theme.

**Who owns the words.** *Mayim* is huge and evenly spread — 581 occurrences, led by Isaiah (56), Genesis (54), Psalms (53). The discriminating words are the source-nouns. *Maqor* ("fountain/spring," H4726) occurs only 18 times, and Proverbs owns it (7), followed by Jeremiah (4), Leviticus (3). Its first canonical appearances are gynecological: Lev 12:7 and 20:18 use *maqor* for "the source of her blood" — the fountain is first a woman's body, only later a metaphor for God (that sequencing is corpus data; what to make of it is inference). *Ma'yan* ("spring," H4599, 23x) opens cosmically — the "fountains of the great deep" of the flood (Gen 7:11; 8:2) — and closes doxologically ("all my springs are in you," Ps 87:7). *Be'er* ("well," H0875, 37x) belongs to Genesis (23 of 37): dug, named, quarreled over. *Bor* ("cistern," H0953) skews to Jeremiah (13 of 26 hits on the gloss "cistern"), where it is both his metaphor (2:13) and his prison (38:6-13) — the prophet of the broken cisterns is himself lowered into one. On the Greek side, *hydōr* (G5204, 79x) is majority-owned by two books: John (24) and Revelation (18) — more than half the NT's water is Johannine. *Pēgē* (G4077) is only 11x: Rev 5, John 3. *Potamos* (G4215, 17x): Rev 8. The NT theme is not diffuse; it is a Johannine possession.

**The grammatical fault line in the Greek.** John's Gospel uses the participle: *hydōr zōn* (John 4:10, ASN participle of *zaō*; genitive *hydatos zōntos* in 7:38) — a direct calque of *mayim chayyim*, water that is itself alive. Revelation never does. Interlinear checks show Rev 7:17 has *zōēs pēgas hydatōn* ("fountains of waters **of life**," noun genitive), and Rev 21:6 and 22:1, 17 likewise use *hydōr zōēs*, "water of life." BSB's "springs of living water" at Rev 7:17 hides this. The participle (water that lives) is confined to Jeremiah's Hebrew, Zechariah, and Jesus's two dialogues; the Apocalypse converts it to a genitive of what the water gives. Whether that shift is theologically loaded is inference; that it is total within the corpus is data.

**Register map.** The same phrase does at least seven different jobs:
- *Property dispute* — Gen 26:19: Isaac's servants dig and find *be'er mayim chayyim*; the herdsmen of Gerar immediately claim it ("The water is ours!"). Living water enters the canon as contested ground.
- *Ritual solvent* — Lev 14:5-6 (cleansing the leper: a bird killed in an earthen vessel *over living water*), Num 19:17 (living water poured over the ashes of the red heifer, the cure for corpse-impurity). In the Torah's cult, living water is the liquid that dissolves death-contact. No metaphor; a recipe.
- *Love lyric* — Song 4:15: the bride is "a garden spring, a well of living water, flowing streams from Lebanon."
- *Covenant lawsuit* — Jer 2:13: "two evils: they have forsaken Me, the fountain of living water, and dug for themselves cisterns — broken cisterns that cannot hold water." Echoed as courtroom shame in Jer 17:13.
- *Eschatological geography* — Zech 14:8 (living waters out from Jerusalem to both seas, "in summer and in winter" — a spring that does not fail in the dry season, which is the point of *chayyim* here); Ezek 47, where the phrase is absent but the verb saturates: "wherever the river flows... swarms of living creatures... everything will live" (47:9).
- *Offer* — John 4:10 ("If you knew the gift [*dōrean*, G1431] of God... He would have given you living water") and the festival shout of John 7:37-38, with the narrator's flat gloss: "He said this about the Spirit" (7:39). The lawsuit's forsaken fountain becomes a thing asked for over a well-curb.
- *Consummation* — Rev 7:17 (the Lamb as shepherd leading to the springs), 21:6 and 22:17 (given "freely" to the thirsty), 22:1 (the river from the throne). The community's own cross-reference data ties these tightly back to Jer 2:13 (top votes: John 4:14, John 7:37, Jer 17:13, Rev 21:6, Ps 36:9).

**The wisdom transposition.** Proverbs uses "fountain of life" (*meqor chayyim*) as a transferable predicate: the mouth of the righteous (10:11), the teaching of the wise (13:14), the fear of YHWH (14:27), understanding (16:22) — and Ps 36:9 gives the un-transposed original: "with **You** is the fountain of life." The Apocrypha runs hard down this track. Sir 15:3: wisdom "will give him water of wisdom to drink." Sir 24:25-31: wisdom is Pishon, Tigris, Euphrates, Jordan, Gihon — Eden's whole river system — and the teacher says "I came out as a canal stream from a river... and my stream became a river, and my river became a sea." Bar 3:12 is Jer 2:13 transposed wholesale into the wisdom key: "You have forsaken the fountain of wisdom." And Sir 24:21 states the exact antithesis of John 4:14: "Those who drink me will be **thirsty for more**," against "whoever drinks the water I give him will **never thirst**." Whether John 4:14 is a deliberate reversal of Sirach is standard-scholarship territory (often argued, not provable from this corpus); that the two sentences contradict verbatim is corpus data.

**Two anomalies, with controls.** (1) In the Jer 2:13 interlinear, "cisterns" appears twice spelled with an aleph — בֹּארֹת (*bōʾrōṯ*) — under lemma H0953 *bor*. Control: Deut 6:11's "cisterns dug out" is plain בֹרֹת, no aleph. The anomalous spelling makes the cistern-word look like the well-word (*be'er*, בְּאֵר) at the very moment the verse opposes cistern to fountain. That the consonantal difference is real, the corpus shows; that it is a scribal pun is my inference, not philology the tools can settle. (2) Jer 17:13 opens "O *miqweh* of Israel" (H4723, glossed "hope," only 5 occurrences, 3 in Jeremiah). Training knowledge says *miqweh* is also a biblical word for a gathering of waters (Gen 1:10; Lev 11:36) and later the ritual bath; if so, "O Pool of Israel... they have forsaken the fountain of living water" is a sustained water-pun. The word_study for H4723 shows only "hope" glosses, so the homograph is presumably tagged under a different Strong's suffix — flagged below as a checkable claim, not asserted.

**The layers beyond the canon.** Patristic: Barnabas 11.1 is the jackpot — an explicit baptismal typology "whether the Lord took any care to foreshadow the water and the cross," quoting Jer 2:13 ("they have forsaken me, a living fountain, and have hewn out for themselves broken cisterns"), then Ps 1's tree by the water-courses and Ezek 47's river with its trees: "we indeed descend into the water full of sins and defilement, but come up bearing fruit in our heart." Yet `get_citations` on Jer 2:13, John 4:10, and Ps 36:9 all return "no patristic citations" — the citation index misses a quotation the full-text search finds (gap logged below). 1 Clement has only creation-waters (33.1); nothing on the theme. Prose classics: Augustine returns to Ps 36:9 like a refrain — "the fountain of life... in Thy light we shall see light" (Conf. 13.5, 13.19, 13.30), "my soul is like a land where no water is" (13.19), and at Ostia he and Monica "gasped with the mouth of our heart after those heavenly streams of Thy fountain, the fountain of life, which is with Thee" (9.23). The Imitation makes the fountain Eucharistic: "the hungering and thirsting to the Fountain of life" (98.2), "Thou art ever a full and overflowing fountain" (100.4), and the striking miniature of 100.5 — barred from the full fountain, "yet will I set my lips to the mouth of the heavenly conduit, that at least I may receive a small drop to quench my thirst, that I dry not up within my heart." Bunyan gives the theme two rivers: the "river of the water of life" whose bank the pilgrims walk (1.642, with marginal citations of Ps 65:9, Rev 22, Ezek 47), and the bridgeless death-river before the gate whose depth varies with faith (1.929-936). Julian inverts the whole current: Christ's "I thirst" opens a "double thirst," and "there is a property in God of thirst and longing... ever He draweth and drinketh, and yet He thirsteth and longeth" (26.2-3; 40.7; 84.2) — in Julian, the fountain is the thirsty one. Chesterton and Brother Lawrence: nothing thematic (Orthodoxy's rivers "run with wine only to make us remember... that they run with water" is about wonder, not this theme; PRESENCE returned zero matches for water OR thirst). Those two silences are results.

**Where the fountain lives.** Tracking the source's address across the corpus: in Jeremiah the fountain is God, and sin is leaving it for self-dug containers. In John 4:14 the fountain migrates: the water "will become **in him** a fount of water springing up to eternal life"; in John 7:38 "out of his belly will flow rivers." In Revelation the spring is back at the throne, and the believer is a drinker again ("let the one who is thirsty come," 22:17). The drinker becomes a source and then, at the end, a guest. Alongside this runs the thirst question: John 4:14 promises "never thirst"; Sir 24:21 promises "thirsty for more"; Julian says even God thirsts until Doomsday; Ps 42:2 and 63:1 make thirst itself the mark of the living ("my soul thirsts for God, the **living** God" — the adjective migrating from the water to its giver).

**Synthesis question.** Does living water end thirst or teach it — is the corpus's promise satisfaction (John 4:14, Rev 21:6) or the transformation of the drinker into a spring who, like her Source, now thirsts (John 7:38, Sir 24:21, Julian 40)?

## Checkable claims

1. The construction *mayim* + *chayyim* (H4325 + H2416a adjacent) occurs at exactly: Gen 26:19; Lev 14:5, 6, 50-52(?); 15:13(?); Num 19:17; Song 4:15; Jer 2:13; 17:13; Zech 14:8. Lev 14:50-52 and Lev 15:13 were **not** interlinear-checked in this survey — verify both contain the phrase (training knowledge says yes).
2. Rev 21:6 and 22:17 use "freely" = *dōrean* (G1432), cognate with the *dōrea* "gift" of John 4:10 (G1431, confirmed in interlinear). Verify the Revelation side with get_interlinear.
3. The water-sense of *miqweh* (Gen 1:10 "gathering of waters"; Lev 11:36 with *ma'yan*) exists in the tagging under some Strong's variant (H4723a?); word_study "H4723" returns only "hope" glosses, 5x. Locate the homograph and confirm the Jer 17:13 pun is corpus-visible.
4. Isa 12:3 ("wells of salvation"), Isa 44:3, 55:1, and Ps 46:4 belong to this theme's hub network (all surfaced via cross-references of John 7:38 or semantic search) but got no word-level study here. Check which well/spring nouns they use.
5. The participle-vs-genitive claim: verify no NT occurrence of *hydōr zōn* outside John 4 and 7, and no *hydōr zōēs* outside Revelation (a G5204+G2198 vs G5204+G2222 co-occurrence sweep would settle it).
6. LXX question the corpus cannot answer (no LXX layer): whether Jer 2:13 LXX renders *mayim chayyim* with *hydōr zōn* — the phrase John's calque would rest on. Flag for a tool that has LXX.
7. Barnabas 11.1's chain quotes ("His water is sure; ye shall see the King" = Isa 33:16-17; the river with trees = Ezek 47) — verify the Isa 33 identification.

## Gaps noticed

- **Citation index misses a real quotation.** `get_citations Jer.2.13` (also John 4:10, Ps 36:9) returns "no patristic citations," yet full-text search finds Barnabas 11.1 quoting Jer 2:13 nearly verbatim. The footnote-extraction pipeline evidently didn't tag it. Suspected bug/coverage gap.
- **Versification, the Isaiah-64 class.** The corpus handles Psalms offsets explicitly (word_study prints "Ps.36.10 = Ps.36.9"). But in the H4726 word study, the sample verse "[Jer.8.23]" printed with **empty text** — MT Jer 8:23 is English 9:1 ("my head waters... a fountain of tears"), so a *maqor* occurrence is effectively invisible in English display. Logged as friction, not fixed.
- **word_study Strong's format.** `H953` and `H875` return "No occurrences"; zero-padded `H0953`/`H0875` work (4-digit `H4325` works unpadded). Pointed-Hebrew lemma query (בְּאֵר) also failed. Cost three wasted calls.
- **Gloss-keyed word_study conflates lemmas.** Query "cistern" merged H0953 with H1356, H1360, and a stray pronoun tag (H3509b), and its sample list included dungeon-verses (Exod 12:29; Jer 37:16) where "cistern" isn't in the English — usable, but counts from gloss-queries are not lemma counts.
- **Semantic hijack observed** (as the skill warns): "the soul's thirst **quenched**" pulled unquenchable-fire verses (Mark 9:48; Ezek 20:48) in hybrid mode. Re-query without the noisy verb was not needed since good hits still dominated, but the effect is real.
- **No LXX layer** (see checkable claim 6); the Hebrew→Johannine bridge for this theme runs through the LXX and cannot be tested here.
- **Thin layers:** 1 Clement effectively silent; Brother Lawrence zero matches; Chesterton only incidental water imagery. Wisdom 11:4-8 (water from flint rock) surfaced but the Exodus rock-water complex (Exod 17, Num 20, Ps 78:16, 114:8, 1 Cor 10:4) was left unstudied — a whole tributary this survey names but does not enter.
