**The core vocabulary and who owns it.** Hebrew *midbar* (H4057) occurs 270 times. Numbers leads with 48, then Exodus 27, Jeremiah 21, Isaiah 21, Psalms 19, Deuteronomy 19, 1 Samuel 18 — the word belongs first to the wandering narrative and second to the prophets who re-read it. Its gloss range is remarkably flat (essentially always "wilderness/desert"): unlike a root with a double life, *midbar* is semantically stable; what varies is what happens there. The near-synonyms divide the labor. *Arabah* (H6160, 61x, Joshua 13 / Deuteronomy 10 lead) is half toponym: glosses split between "the.plains" (15), "Arabah" (15), and "desert.plain" — a word that names a specific rift-valley geography, which is why it can stand in poetic parallel to *midbar* (Isa 40:3). *Yeshimon* (H3452, 13x) likewise oscillates between place-name "Jeshimon" (6) and common noun "desolate place" (6) — the Ziphite verses (1Sam 23:19, 23:24, 26:1, 26:3) treat it as a landmark David hides behind. *Tsiyyah* (H6723, 16x) is the dryness word ("of.dryness" 9 of 16), at home in poetry: Ps 63:1 "a dry and weary land, where there is no water"; Isa 53:2 the servant as "a root out of dry ground." *Tohu* (H8414, 20x, Isaiah owns 11) drags wilderness toward cosmology: the same word is Gen 1:2's "formless" and Deut 32:10's "howling wilderness." And *shemamah* (H8077, 56x, Ezekiel 21 / Jeremiah 15) is not a place at all but a verdict — "a.desolation" (26), what a land *becomes* under judgment (Lev 26:33; Isa 1:7; Jer 6:8).

**Deut 32:10 stacks the field in one line.** The interlinear shows *ʾereṣ midbar* + *tohu* + *yeshimon* in sequence: "in a land of wilderness, and in a wasteland, a howling, of a desolate place — he surrounded it, he took care of it, he guarded it like the pupil of his eye." Three wilderness words piled up precisely where the tenderness is maximal. The corpus's own juxtaposition, not mine.

**The wilderness/word braid.** The traditional link of *midbar* to *dabar* "speak" is folk etymology, not philology — I flag it as tradition. But the corpus data underneath it is real and checkable: *dabar* (H1696, 1136x) has its single largest book concentration in Numbers (119), the wilderness book, ahead of Jeremiah (114) and Exodus (87). Deut 8:3 makes the collocation explicit: in the wilderness man lives "by every utterance of the mouth of Yahweh" (*kol-motsaʾ pi-YHWH*). And MT Hos 2:16 (= English 2:14) sets the verb inside the noun: *veholachtiha hammidbar vedibbarti al-libbah* — "I will lead her [to] the wilderness and I will *speak* to her heart." Whatever the roots' history, the corpus keeps putting the divine voice in the empty place.

**Isa 40:3 — where "in the wilderness" attaches.** The MT interlinear reads: *qol qoreʾ* ("a voice [is] calling"), then *bammidbar pannu derekh YHWH* — "in the wilderness clear the way of Yahweh," with the parallel colon "in the *arabah* a highway for our God." The Hebrew parallelism binds "in the wilderness" to the road-building, not the voice, and both BSB and WEB translate accordingly (compare_versions confirms both: "Prepare the way... in the wilderness"). All three Gospel quotations checked (Matt 3:3, Mark 1:3, John 1:23) attach it instead to the voice: "the voice of one crying in the wilderness." That the Gospels follow the LXX's construal is standard scholarship; the corpus has no LXX text, so I could verify the MT and NT poles but not the middle term (gap, below). Either way both readings are wilderness readings: someone — the herald or the road crew — is out there.

**The Greek side splits noun from verb.** *Erēmos* (G2048, 47x, Luke 10 / Mark 9 / Acts 9) is formally an adjective, and its gloss range shows it: "wilderness" (31) but also "solitary" and — in Gal 4:27, quoting Isa 54:1 — "desolate woman": "more are the children of the desolate woman than of her who has a husband." That single gloss ties the Greek word straight into the Hebrew *shemamah*→marriage arc of Isa 62:4 ("No longer will you be called... Desolate; you will be called Hephzibah... Beulah"). Meanwhile the verb *erēmoō* (G2049, 5x) and noun *erēmōsis* (G2050, 3x) are uniformly negative: the divided kingdom "brought to desolation" (Matt 12:25), Babylon made desolate (Rev 17:16, 18:17-19), the "abomination of desolation" (Matt 24:15; Mark 13:14; Luke 21:20). The place-word hosts theophany, testing, feeding, refuge; the action-word family only ever destroys. *Erēmia* (G2047, 4x) covers the feeding-miracle question ("where in this desolate place could we find bread," Matt 15:33) and Heb 11:38's refugees "in deserts and mountains."

**Register map.** (a) *Rendezvous*: the wilderness as where God is met — Moses at Horeb "on the far side of the wilderness" (Exod 3:1); the Exodus demand itself, "let us take a three-day journey into the wilderness, so that we may sacrifice" (Exod 5:1, 5:3, 7:16). (b) *Testing/humbling*: Deut 8:2 — led "forty years... so that He might humble you and test you in order to know what was in your heart"; the interlinear shows the triad *ʿanah* (humble), *nasah* (test), *yadaʿ* (know); cross-references pull Exod 16:4 (manna "that I may test them") and land in Jas 1:3 / 1 Pet 1:7. (c) *Rebellion/grief*: Ps 78:40 "How often they disobeyed Him in the wilderness"; Ps 106:14; Ezek 20:36 extends it forward — "I will bring you into the wilderness of the nations, where I will enter into judgment with you face to face" — a second, forensic exodus. (d) *Betrothal*: Hos 2:14 (allure, speak to her heart), answered by Jer 2:2 "your love as a bride, how you followed Me in the wilderness, in a land not sown" — find_similar(Hos 2:14) surfaced Jer 2:2 unprompted. Deut 32:10's "pupil of his eye" belongs here too. (e) *Refuge*: Hagar found by the spring (Gen 16:7); Ishmael the wilderness archer (Gen 21:20); David south of Jeshimon; 1 Macc 2:29 "many who sought justice and judgment went down into the wilderness to live there"; Rev 12:6 the woman flees "into the wilderness, where God had prepared a place for her." (f) *Judgment/desolation*: the whole *shemamah* file; 1 Macc 1:39 "her sanctuary was laid waste like a wilderness." (g) *Transformation*: Ps 107 runs the machine both directions in one psalm — "He turns rivers into deserts" (v.33) and "He turns a desert into a pool of water" (v.35); Isa 35:1, 41:18, 43:19 promise the reversal. (h) *Withdrawal*: the NT's quiet register — Jesus "went out to a solitary place [erēmos] to pray" (Mark 1:35; 6:31), a chosen, temporary wilderness. Mark 1:12-13 compresses several registers into two verses: driven (*ekballei*) by the Spirit, tempted by Satan, "with the wild animals," served by angels.

**Entities.** Sinai (18 mentions, concentrated Exod-Num), Kadesh/Kadesh-barnea (15+10 — the graph splits the alias into two entities, worth knowing downstream), Paran (8: Ishmael's home Gen 21:21, the spies' base Num 13:3, 13:26, and David's retreat 1Sam 25:1 — one wilderness hosting three different stories). Numbers 20 alone links Kadesh, Meribah, Zin, Mount Hor, Edom: the chapter where Miriam dies, Moses strikes the rock, and Aaron dies — the wilderness as the place the founding generation is buried.

**Apocrypha.** Three distinct retellings. Wisdom 11:2-4 makes Wisdom the guide: "They traveled through a desert without inhabitant... they thirsted... and water was given to them out of the flinty rock." 2 Esdras 1:17-22 re-runs the murmuring as indictment ("Why have you brought us into this wilderness to kill us?"), and 9:29 as nostalgia ("you showed yourself among us... in the wilderness... where no man treads and that bears no fruit"). 1 Maccabees is tactical: the wilderness is where the faithful flee (2:29-31), armies march (5:24, 5:28), and Jonathan camps (9:33, 9:62) — refuge militarized. That this anticipates the Qumran move is my inference from training knowledge, not a corpus datum.

**Patristic layer.** 1 Clement: zero hits for wilderness/desert — a genuine gap-finding for a text so fond of Exodus examples. Barnabas gives two: the scapegoat "driven into the wilderness" read as a type of Jesus (BARNABAS.7.1), and a direct quotation, "Hear, ye children, the voice of one crying in the wilderness" (BARNABAS.9.1). Yet get_citations(Isa 40:3) returns "no patristic citations" — the quotation exists in the text but is not in the citation index (logged below).

**Prose classics.** Bunyan opens the entire allegory inside the theme: "As I walked through the wilderness of this world..." (PILGRIM.1.41) — wilderness as the frame for everything, not an episode; and he glosses the Valley of the Shadow of Death with Jer 2:6 verbatim (PILGRIM.1.360). Augustine gives the monastic register secondhand — "the fruitful deserts of the wilderness" and Antony's life (CONFESSIONS.8.16) — and a personal one: "I had purposed to flee to the wilderness: but Thou forbadest me" (10.70), plus an interiorized "so vast wilderness, full of snares" (10.56). Julian of Norwich performs the survey's most startling inversion: in the lord-and-servant shewing the *Lord* sits "on the earth, barren and desert, alone in wilderness," waiting in fallen man's soul, and "without the Lord was nothing but wilderness" (JULIAN.60.14, 60.20) — until "now sitteth not the Son on earth in wilderness" (60.29). In Julian, God is the one in the wilderness, and the wilderness is us. Chesterton contributes two asides: "a mere unmeaning wilderness is not even impressive" (ORTHODOXY.9.20) and the Decalogue as "regimental orders, issued to protect a certain ark across a certain desert" (5.5). The Imitation has almost nothing: its "desert(s)" hits are mostly *desertion* and *deserts*-as-merits — an English-homograph artifact of stemmed search, with only 18.2's hermits ("the holy fathers who dwelt in the desert") on theme.

**Synthesis question.** The corpus pairs the wilderness with the voice at every layer — *dabar* densest in Numbers, "every utterance of the mouth of Yahweh" as wilderness food, "I will speak to her heart" in the *midbar*, the herald of Isa 40:3, John's word "came... in the wilderness" (Luke 3:2) — while the *erēmoō* verb family means being emptied: **is emptiness the corpus's precondition for address — must a place (or a people) first be un-inhabited before it can be spoken to?**

## Checkable claims

1. *midbar* H4057 = 270 occurrences, Numbers leading at 48 (verified this run; re-runnable via word_study H4057).
2. *dabar* H1696's largest single-book count is Numbers (119 of 1136) — verified as raw count; the *density* claim (vs. shorter books) would need word-count normalization the tools don't provide.
3. MT Hos 2:16 contains both *hammidbar* and *dibbarti* (verified via get_interlinear "Hosea 2:16"; note the versification trap below).
4. Gal 4:27's "desolate woman" is *erēmos* G2048 — asserted from the word_study gloss list; verify with get_interlinear Galatians 4:27.
5. Barnabas 9 quotes Isa 40:3 in text, but get_citations("Isaiah 40:3") returns nothing — verify by re-reading BARNABAS.9.1; suspected citation-index miss.
6. Rev 12:6's "place prepared" (*hētoimasmenon*?) may share the verb of "make ready the way" (*hetoimasate*, Mark 1:3) — single-word link, checkable via get_interlinear on both verses; not yet checked.
7. The Gospels' attachment of "in the wilderness" to the voice (vs. MT's attachment to the way) is verified for Matt 3:3, Mark 1:3, John 1:23; the claim that this follows the LXX is training knowledge — no LXX in corpus to check.
8. word_study(H4057) lists Exod.8.23-24 among sample verses, but the displayed English (flies) contains no wilderness; English Exod 8:28 ("sacrifice... in the wilderness") = MT 8:24 — verify the Exodus 8 offset via get_interlinear "Exodus 8:24".

## Gaps noticed

- **Hosea 2 versification offset, confirmed live.** get_interlinear("Hosea 2:14") displays the English 2:14 header ("I will allure her") over Hebrew that is actually MT 2:14 = English 2:12 (devastating vines and fig trees). Control: get_interlinear("Hosea 2:16") shows English 2:16's header over MT 2:16 = English 2:14's Hebrew (the allure verse). The tool indexes Hebrew by MT numbering and English by English numbering; in Hosea 2 they diverge by 2. Psalms offsets are handled explicitly (word_study samples print "Ps.68.8 = Ps.68.7"), but Hosea is not.
- **Same offset class visible in word_study samples for Exodus 8** (H4057 sample at Exod.8.23-24 whose English text lacks the word entirely). The offset problem is not Hosea-specific.
- **No LXX layer.** The MT-vs-LXX question at the heart of Isa 40:3's reception cannot be closed inside the corpus.
- **Citation index is thin.** get_citations returned nothing for Isa 40:3 (despite Barnabas quoting it) or Ps 95:8; patristic citations currently cover only 1 Clement and Barnabas, and apparently not all their quotations.
- **1 Clement has no wilderness material** — a real distribution finding, given its Exodus-heavy exempla.
- **No desert-fathers text in the prose corpus.** The Christian tradition most centered on this theme (Antony, the Apophthegmata) is present only secondhand through Confessions 8.16 — a corpus-acquisition candidate.
- **Stemmed-search homograph noise**: in prose layers "desert" matches *deserted/desertion* and *deserts* (= merits); most IMITATION hits were off-theme. A search-side language filter would help.
- **Mild lexical hijack in hybrid semantic_search**: the word "beloved" in the Hos 2:14-flavored query pulled epistolary "beloved" addresses (1 John 3:21, Col 3:12) above genuine parallels.
- **Entity graph splits Kadesh and Kadesh-barnea** into separate place entities with disjoint mention lists — downstream users should merge them.
